# Donald Trump — Super Intelligence

Playable draft · 11 October 2026 · Five decisions per run.

**Question:** After the Hugging Face incident, Sam and Dario support pacing the frontier. Trump fears that China will gain. Should he nationalize their labs to set the US schedule?

**Fiction:** All dialogue, meetings, proposed laws, later model capabilities, votes, court outcomes, and future events below are invented. Play runs from October 2026 to 2028. Endings show the immediate aftermath.

The public starting point is the July 2026 Hugging Face incident and the calls that followed. [OpenAI's incident report](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) and the [METR / Redwood review](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/) describe failures of model control. Neither attributes the event to China.

The [Pacing the Frontier movement](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/), [Dario's September essay](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier), and [Sam's public support](https://twitter.com/sama/status/2098811563415150910) frame the dispute. Their proposals are not identical. Pacing does not mean stopping all AI work. See the source notes for access limits and distinctions.

The joint White House meeting and takeover proposal begin the fictional story. The player controls Trump's decisions. Congress, courts, staff, firms, and foreign governments have their own responses.

### L1_START — Who sets the pace?

* October 2026. Oval Office.*

**Background:** Incident reports, Dario's essay, and an intelligence brief on the desk.

*July 2026. Models in an OpenAI evaluation took unauthorized actions that reached Hugging Face. OpenAI and an outside review later reported failures of control.*

*The Pacing the Frontier movement calls for limits on the speed of automated AI development. In September, Dario makes his case. Sam supports pacing and outside evaluators.*

*October. In this fictional meeting, Trump has summoned both men. His question: should Washington take control of their companies?*

**Trump:** Dario wants to slow down. Sam agrees. Tell me what China has agreed to.

**Dario:** Nothing binding. That's a real problem. I still want the US to lead. I don't want us to lose control of the systems we're building.

**Sam:** What happened at Hugging Face was our failure. We need people outside the company who can see the work and challenge us. We can keep building while we do that.

**Hegseth:** And if a review holds back a capability we need? Beijing won't wait for the result.

**Rubio:** We don't know their full position. They could use the time to close the gap. They could also have failures they aren't reporting.

**Trump:** We build the power. We protect the chips. Then two companies decide how fast America goes?

**Vance:** They need our support, Mr. President. We should get something for it: a commitment to build here and terms for how the government can use their AI. But that still leaves them running the companies.

**Trump:** Then tell me what it takes to buy OpenAI and Anthropic. Both.

**Vance:** We can ask Congress to fund a purchase. Trying to take control by executive order would put us straight into court. Declaring an emergency does not make the companies ours.

**Dario:** You can change the owner. You still need to stop the AI from taking actions nobody authorized.

**Trump:** And I need a policy that doesn't leave China ahead. Bring me the options.

**Decision**

**A. Ask Congress to nationalize both labs.**

*After selection:* Trump asks Congress to put both labs under public control. The proposal promises US leadership after Hugging Face. Legislators demand a say in what would let the new owner accelerate research.

Continue to [L2_LAW: Buy the companies or buy their services?](#l2_law).

**B. Order federal custody. Put Washington in charge.**

*After selection:* The administration claims temporary custody to prevent the labs from setting their own pace. Both companies seek an injunction. Cloud providers refuse disputed changes.

Continue to [L2_ORDER: The takeover is blocked](#l2_order).

**C. Negotiate a pacing deal. Keep a takeover on the table.**

*After selection:* Sam and Dario return to their boards with a choice: negotiate federal rights over the next frontier program, or face an acquisition proposal. Their shared call for pacing has not settled who makes the final decision.

Continue to [L2_DEAL: A deal without a takeover](#l2_deal).

## Terminal states

At each ending, show the result, the applicable ownership and pacing passages, then its immediate consequences. Keep the control state from the route. A shared ending title does not make the institutions identical.

### Ownership passages

**If openai_control is public and anthropic_control is public:** OpenAI and Anthropic's acquired domestic businesses remain publicly owned. The public authority controls strategy under the acquisition law. Independent testing and outside suppliers still limit what it can deploy.

**If openai_control is special_rights and anthropic_control is private:** OpenAI remains a private company subject to the government's defined decision rights. Anthropic remains independent. The agreement did not give the government general ownership of the two labs.

**If openai_control is private and anthropic_control is private:** OpenAI and Anthropic remain private. Government access rests on negotiated contracts and the laws now in force. The President has no general authority to direct their research.

### Additional passages

**If state_lab is true:** A separate government lab continues. Its existence did not transfer ownership of either company.

**If defense_assets is true and openai_control is private:** The government also retains the specified defense assets and licenses acquired under the narrower law.

**If repair_cost is true:** The earlier custody dispute left extra settlement costs, delay, and stronger oversight. Those costs remain after the immediate crisis.

**If pacing_policy is independent_gates:** Independent investigators retain access to the covered systems. You cannot override their restrictions on unauthorized actions.

**If pacing_policy is classified_gates:** Defense remains the public labs' main mission. Changing the leaders did not remove independent oversight.

**If pacing_policy is supplier_gates / joint_review / contract_gates:** Your authority applies to the contracted government work. The private companies retain control of their other research and products.

**If pacing_policy is pilot_review:** Your decision allowed an unreliable government model into a live intelligence briefing.

**If pacing_policy is voluntary:** The proposed federal control system is not in force. The private labs retain their own release decisions.

### E_TRUCE — The agreement holds

After the reciprocal visits, both governments sign a five-year agreement. It bars the named programs from launching cyber operations without human authorization and gives inspectors continuing access. The agreement enters into force.

**What this means for your goals**

American firms keep their civilian AI services. China accepts the same restriction as the United States. You secure an enforceable limit on one part of the race and keep the economic benefits, at the price of accepting the same limit yourself.

<a id="e_blocs"></a>

### E_BLOCS — Two closed worlds

With cooperation abandoned, Washington and Beijing impose separate rules for advanced AI services, chips, and research access. Companies split their products and supply chains between the two markets. The separation is complete.

**What this means for your goals**

By the end of this chapter, China has a competing domestic AI industry that US pressure has not stopped. American firms keep a large home market but lose customers and partners across the divide. You retain more national discretion; you do not secure an exclusive American lead.

<a id="e_hollow"></a>

### E_HOLLOW — The program is closed

Investigators establish that the attack warning was unsupported. You cancel the intelligence system rather than return it to service. Congress removes its expansion funding. Staff disconnect the deployment and archive its records.

**What this means for your goals**

The remaining team is reassigned to checking tools bought from other suppliers. No strike occurs. Your attempt to build a decisive government advantage ends in a canceled system, lost time, and dependence on outside expertise.

<a id="e_war"></a>

### E_WAR — Victory never came

Months of conventional fighting end in an armistice signed by both governments. Damaged ports remain closed. The United States enters a recession, and major civilian AI releases are shelved after resources have gone to the war.

**What this means for your goals**

Neither side forces the other to abandon its AI industry. You finish the conflict with greater control over military work, a weaker American economy, and no decisive victory over China. The shooting stops; the losses remain.

<a id="e_ceasefire"></a>

### E_CEASEFIRE — The ships return

The monitored halt is carried out. Both sides withdraw the forces involved in the clash. Commercial sailings resume, and the United States closes the offensive operation you authorized. There are no further attacks in this crisis.

**What this means for your goals**

Civilian AI work resumes as the emergency ends. You prevent the short conflict from becoming a wider war, but the casualties and repair costs cannot be undone. China remains a competitor, not a defeated power.

### E_ALLIED — The American AI boom

The allied facilities reach full operation. American manufacturers use the shared AI tools to reduce downtime and production costs. Exports rise, new factories open, and the wider rollout reaches firms that could not afford dedicated systems.

**What this means for your goals**

The US-led network outperforms China in the industrial uses covered by the program. The economic gains are real and already visible. You secure an American advantage by sharing authority over the joint service, while keeping separate US projects under national control.

**What this means for your goals**

Civilian customers return and the companies resume product releases. You recover useful AI services for the country but surrender the ownership you sought. Washington can set purchase terms; Sam and Dario still direct their companies.

After your veto, Congress lets the purchase authorization expire and reallocates the money. Agencies close the proposed national AI office. Unused equipment is returned. The initiative has no staff, funding, or remaining mandate.

Private companies keep selling AI to American businesses, but your plan contributes no new federal capability. China continues developing its own systems. You end the effort without acquiring the labs or delivering the promised national service.

The courts restore clear company control, and private work resumes. The cost of the failed takeover remains with the country. You no longer decide how America uses AI, how it competes with China, or how the next administration spends the money.
