# Open questions and update queue

The items below are limits of this corpus.
They are not claims that the information cannot exist.

| Priority | Question | Why it matters | Next evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Who holds each command and cabinet role at game start? | A dated profile may be stale. | Official appointment and removal records. |
| 1 | What rules govern the specific chip shipment? | Licenses can depend on product, buyer, date, and end use. | Current rule text and license conditions. |
| 1 | What can each AI deployment actually do? | A benchmark does not establish field reliability. | Independent tests with tool and access details. |
| 1 | Who authorized a reported AI incident? | Origin does not establish state responsibility. | Independent incident findings and corroborated attribution. |
| 1 | Which Taiwan policy could interrupt deliveries? | An election alone does not establish that mechanism. | Proposed law, voting record, court review, and firm response. |
| 2 | How much usable compute can each actor access? | Chip stock is only one input. | Dated capacity, utilization, power, and import data. |
| 2 | What is usable overseas chip capacity by process and package? | Announced investment is not equivalent output. | Production, qualification, yield, and packaging records. |
| 2 | Which military AI systems have passed field tests? | Procurement and intent can exceed deployment. | Public evaluation and operational evidence. |
| 2 | How do Chinese practitioners assess restraint? | English-language sources can skew the corpus. | More original Chinese papers and official transcripts. |
| 2 | What channels work during a crisis? | A listed hotline may not be answered. | Exercises, readouts, and use during incidents. |
| 2 | What outcomes would change an ally's response? | Alliance membership does not imply automatic action. | Domestic authority, consultations, and public commitments. |
| 3 | How will AI affect domestic legitimacy and employment? | These can alter risk tolerance and investment. | Country-specific empirical studies. |
| 3 | How do finance, insurance, shipping, water, and energy interact? | A chip shock can spread without direct military damage. | Firm disclosures and sector-specific models. |
| 3 | How do India, Singapore, Gulf states, and Europe affect access? | Third countries can change markets and diplomacy. | More local sources and compute policy records. |
| 3 | How should game probabilities be set? | No common calibrated conflict model is established here. | Defined questions, expert elicitation, and backtesting. |

## Coverage limits

Military capabilities use broad public categories.
The corpus does not provide a complete order of battle.
Secondary states and suppliers have less detail than the core actors.
Private bargaining, classified capabilities, and unpublished model behavior remain unknown.
The corpus includes both current and historical public figures.
Their authority must follow the dated role record.

Research streams can cite the same source.
The source index keeps both IDs and reports duplicate URLs.
This preserves provenance but does not add independent evidence.

## Before the first game

Choose a start date and an explicit starting scenario.
Refresh the highest-priority records.
Select the playable roles and the decisions each role can make.
Set turn length and the information delay rules.
Keep any invented rates and weights in a separate calibration file.
Test peaceful and adverse paths with the same rules.
