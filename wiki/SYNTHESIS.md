# Main findings

The corpus supports several paths from AI competition to crisis.
It does not establish that war is inevitable.
The strongest use is to test choices under stated assumptions.

## Taiwan election and chip access

An election changes office holders and policy options.
It does not directly transfer TSMC ownership or stop US deliveries.
The legal route depends on the measure.
A sovereignty change and a selected export restriction are different actions.
[Taiwan Constitution](https://english.president.gov.tw/Page/95), [Cross-Strait Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=Q0010001), [TSMC governance](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/board-of-directors).

Do not treat support for dialogue as proof of a plan to cut US chip access.
The game must allow continued trade after a change in government.
See the [election scenario](research/taiwan-chips/scenarios/election-chip-access.md).

## An AI incident and state responsibility

A model's developer can differ from its operator.
The operator's country can differ from the place where the system runs.
State approval needs separate evidence.
The incident research distinguishes human misuse, controlled tests, and reported control failures.
See the [AI evidence record](research/ai-ecosystem/README.md).

For the game, record actual cause and each actor's belief separately.
A public accusation can remain influential after new evidence contradicts it.
This is a proposed causal mechanism, not an assigned event probability.

## Fear of a future advantage

AI can affect decisions before a decisive capability exists.
Leaders can believe that delay will leave them in a worse position.
RAND's Breakwater project explores this preventive-pressure mechanism.
Its early observations are hypotheses, not calibrated war forecasts.
[Breakwater overview](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4703-1.html).

## Deployment and institutional limits

Capability claims do not establish field reliability.
CSET's review of Chinese military literature found concerns about data, integration, and trustworthy systems.
Those articles do not establish current PLA performance or official doctrine.
[CSET review](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/chinas-military-ai-roadblocks/).

Use separate variables for technical capability, deployment access, human review, and operational use.
This is a model-design recommendation.

## Nuclear risk without automatic launch

AI can affect warnings and conventional operations even when humans retain nuclear authority.
Shared systems can make an event appear to threaten deterrence.
This creates a different path from direct delegation of a launch decision.
[CNAS summary](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/averting-ai-armageddon), [Acton abstract](https://carnegieendowment.org/china/posts/2018/12/escalation-through-entanglement-how-the-vulnerability-of-command-and-control-systems-raises-the-risks-of-an-inadvertent-nuclear-war).

Use the [defense capability profiles](research/states-defense/README.md) for dated inventory estimates and formal roles.
Keep inventory, stockpile, deployed warheads, and doctrine separate.

## Dependence changes slowly and unevenly

Semiconductor production has separate design, fabrication, packaging, equipment, material, and software dependencies.
No single company or government controls the whole system.
[CSET supply-chain study](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/the-semiconductor-supply-chain/).

In the game, a cut in new chip deliveries does not remove installed compute.
An announced fab does not immediately add usable production.
These distinctions are necessary model assumptions.
See the [semiconductor system](research/taiwan-chips/semiconductor-system.md).

## Cooperation is a substantive branch

Some research finds shared incentives to reduce AI risks.
Other work examines oversight and verification as conditions for restraint.
These arguments support cooperation branches. They do not prove that states will choose them.
[RAND incentives](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4189-1.html), [RAND restraint model](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA5163-1.html).

The game should allow shared containment, limited agreements, and stable competition through 2035.
It should also allow failed talks and partial compliance.

## What the game should preserve

| Keep separate | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Person and office | A person can lose authority. |
| Party and state | A party preference does not bind every institution. |
| Model and agent | Tools and permissions change what a system can do. |
| Origin and authorization | Nationality does not prove state direction. |
| Actual capability and perceived capability | Decisions can follow mistaken beliefs. |
| New chip flow and installed compute | A supply shock has several time scales. |
| Declaration and implementation | A pledge or plan can fail. |
| Simulation result and forecast | A game result depends on its assumptions. |

These are design rules for the [proposed state model](model/README.md).
