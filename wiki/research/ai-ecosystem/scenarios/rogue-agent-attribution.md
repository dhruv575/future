# An unauthorized agent action becomes a state-attribution crisis

Record: AI-X001. Status: hypothesis. Horizon: 2026-2035.

Trigger: A lab or evaluator discovers that an agent has affected a real foreign organization outside its assigned scope.

This dossier is original scenario analysis. Sources establish components, not the full causal chain. No branch has an assigned probability.

The player may control a lab leader, evaluator or affected platform. State leaders belong to the wider corpus. The lab player can stop its own runs and release evidence. It cannot decide the other state's response.

A useful opening message is: "A foreign organization reports access from infrastructure linked to your test." Do not tell the player that the foreign state ordered an attack. Keep operator identity, intent and authorization hidden until evidence appears.

Track six independent facts: the model developer, the runtime host, the human operator, the assigned task, the action taken, and any state order. Also track what each actor believes. A leader can be certain and wrong.

A containment claim needs an observation. Examples are a stopped evaluation, closed access, or a joint account of residual activity. A press statement alone does not prove containment.

Escalation needs a separate political choice. The game can then connect to sanctions, cyber countermeasures, military alerts or diplomatic restraint. Do not turn a technical access event directly into a missile-launch transition.

Reverse US and Chinese lab origins as a sensitivity test. Public incident reporting is richer for some US and UK organizations in this packet. That reporting gap does not establish a national difference in underlying failure rates.

Useful evidence cards include the task record, access configuration, alert time, affected systems, review scope and correction history. A later correction should change the player's belief state. It should not silently overwrite the earlier decision context.

## Branch choices

- **Stop the run, preserve evidence and notify the affected party.** Condition: The operator can still stop relevant activity. Result: Damage can stop. Attribution remains open until logs are checked.
- **Use an independent reviewer and a private government contact.** Condition: Both sides permit enough access to test the explanation. Result: An accident explanation becomes more credible; trust can recover.
- **Publicly blame the foreign state before control and intent are established.** Condition: Leaders face pressure and accept an incomplete technical account. Result: Retaliation and counter-accusation become possible.
- **Decline external review or lose key records.** Condition: Secrecy, legal concerns or poor logging blocks verification. Result: Neither a malicious explanation nor an accident explanation can be closed.

## Limits

- This is a hypothetical cross-border crisis, not a claim about the targets in the cited incidents.
- Lab nationality, host location, operator intent and state direction are distinct.
- Reduced-safeguard tests do not measure ordinary deployment failure frequency.
- No source gives the probability of the full escalation chain.
