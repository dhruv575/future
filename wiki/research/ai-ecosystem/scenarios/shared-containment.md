# A shared AI incident creates a safety bargain

Record: AI-X005. Status: hypothesis. Horizon: 2026-2035.

Trigger: A serious incident affects several jurisdictions and produces evidence that all major developers face a related risk.

This dossier is original scenario analysis. Sources establish components, not the full causal chain. No branch has an assigned probability.

The game needs a successful containment path. An incident can create a shared interest in limiting harm even when actors compete in other areas.

The 2025 Anthropic-OpenAI exercise is a narrow precedent for rival firms testing each other's systems. The 2026 Singapore Consensus is a separate example of an international technical research agenda. Neither establishes a binding state agreement. See [AI-S013](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/openai-findings/) and [AI-S040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14611).

Amodei's pacing proposal offers candidate policy actions, including embedded evaluators and wider coordination. Treat it as a public position from a participant with commercial and political interests. Do not import his warning horizon as the game's event clock. See [AI-S014](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier).

A bargain needs a defined subject. Possible subjects are evaluation access, incident notification, limits on risky internal runs, protection of civilian infrastructure, or shared defensive research. Their authority, verification cost and scope differ.

For an evaluator player, choices concern access requirements, publication limits and confidence statements. For a lab player, choices concern logs, test configurations and verified shutdown of specific runs. Government players decide whether a technical agreement changes their strategic stance.

Track commitments and implementation separately. A signed pledge is not a verified test result. A patch offered is not a patch installed. A training pause is not a guarantee that independent copies have stopped operating.

Anthropic's defense forecast is a useful disagreement card: it expects an advantage for defenders within two years, but says near-term patching is slow. The game can vary deployment capacity without asserting that offense or defense must always win. See [AI-S036](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission).

## Branch choices

- **Share bounded evidence through independent evaluators.** Condition: Parties accept access rules and protect unrelated secrets. Result: A common technical account becomes more plausible.
- **Adopt common evaluation and incident-reporting terms.** Condition: Labs and governments agree on scope and verification. Result: Future incidents can be easier to compare and contain.
- **Focus on defensive deployment while research continues under controls.** Condition: Defenders can test and safely apply fixes. Result: Resilience may improve without a full research halt.
- **Keep risk findings secret to protect competitive advantage.** Condition: Actors see disclosure as too costly. Result: Common failure modes can remain hidden and distrust can persist.

## Limits

- This is a candidate cooperation pathway, not a prediction of a US-China agreement.
- Company cross-evaluation does not settle state verification problems.
- Participant proposals for pacing are not implemented treaties.
- Defensive access helps only if fixes are validated and deployed.
