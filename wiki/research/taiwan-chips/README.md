## Main conclusions

An election result is one event. A chip cutoff requires further policy and implementation steps. Engagement with Beijing is not proof of a cutoff policy. The KMT's stated position is one source of counterevidence. [AP, 2026-06-12](https://apnews.com/article/taiwan-opposition-leader-china-trump-13c4c9c7f1bb0b358a4b3df1ceea0bf0).

Chip supply has many stages. A fab, a company, its IP, its skilled staff and the right to sell output are separate assets. The CSET taxonomy helps define this system. [CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/the-semiconductor-supply-chain/).

The silicon-shield argument is contested. Economic cost can support restraint. It is not a guarantee against force. [CFR task force](https://www.cfr.org/task-force-reports/us-taiwan-relations-in-a-new-era/cdn/ff/Ig_ZOuCbVzeNEasQrQ3Evyn2UdT3rn-2TreCp8i-BqE/1687531766/public/2023-06/TFR81_U.S.-TaiwanRelationsNewEra_SinglePages_2023-06-05_Online.pdf).
