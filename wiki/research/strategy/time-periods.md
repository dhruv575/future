# Time periods through 2035

These periods organize scenarios. They are not predictions.

| Period | Main design questions | What must remain uncertain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cutoff: 2026-10-10 | Which dated facts can support the starting state? | Private plans, classified systems, and incomplete incident findings. |
| 2027–2029 | How do elections, licenses, deployment choices, and AI incidents interact? | Election results, office holders, model progress, and state responses. |
| 2030–2032 | Do new capacity and institutions change dependence and risk? | Completion dates, usable output, military integration, and compliance. |
| 2033–2035 | Does competition settle, fragment, or produce repeated crises? | Technical progress, political continuity, and lasting AI advantage. |

## Timing rules

Use hours or days for an incident.
Use months for policy and procurement choices.
Use years for major infrastructure change.
These are design defaults, not measured universal durations.

An emergency can interrupt a long project.
A later recovery does not erase earlier harm.
Keep a dated event log so both effects remain visible.

## Resources and delay

The IEA's 2025 scenarios show a wide range of data-center electricity demand by 2035.
The source also describes grid delays.
Use this to test resource constraints, not to set a fixed compute ceiling.
[IEA outlook](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary).

## People over time

A 2026 profile cannot establish who holds office in 2032.
The game can use real people in a dated starting scenario.
For later appointments, use an explicit scenario assignment or an unnamed office holder.
Keep the person record separate from the office's powers.
