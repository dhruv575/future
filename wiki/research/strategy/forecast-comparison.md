# Forecasts are not interchangeable

| Source | What it describes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/) | A detailed scenario with alternative endings. | A settled AGI date or the chance of US–China war. |
| [Situational Awareness](https://situational-awareness.ai/) | One author's rapid-progress forecast and strategic argument. | Consensus, certain scaling gains, or a guaranteed first-mover advantage. |
| [Grace et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843) | Aggregated researcher forecasts for defined capabilities. | A prediction from a representative sample of decision-makers. |
| [RAND futures](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3034-2.html) | Several conditional geopolitical futures. | Relative probabilities for those futures. |
| [IEA Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary) | Energy demand under stated adoption and infrastructure assumptions. | A direct conversion from electricity use to intelligence. |
| [Superintelligence Strategy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05628) | An argument for a strategic policy framework. | An adopted government policy or tested deterrence system. |

## Comparison rules

The corpus does not average these forecasts.
Their definitions, methods, horizons, and incentives differ.
The [prediction record](../../PREDICTIONS.md) preserves those differences.

The Grace survey's all-task definition is broader than many uses of AGI.
The survey also gives a 50% aggregate horizon of 2047 for that outcome.
This is outside the game horizon, but it shows why a slower branch is needed.
[Source abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843).

AI 2027 later clarified that its title year was the authors' modal year at publication.
It was not their median deadline.
The page links to newer forecasts.
Preserve the dated scenario when assessing what it predicted.
[Author clarification](https://ai-2027.com/).

## Proposed uncertainty branches

These are design choices. No branch weight is assigned.

1. Capability improves slowly. Integration costs remain large.
2. Capability improves quickly. Real-world deployment remains uneven.
3. AI research automation speeds further capability growth.
4. Resource limits, security failures, or policy slow expansion.
5. Similar capabilities spread to several states and firms.

Run the same political flashpoint under several branches.
This can reveal whether an outcome depends on one strong technical assumption.
