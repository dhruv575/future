# Dario: research constraints for the next story discussion

Status: **writer inference**. A [first playable draft](../../../docs/narrative/dario-the-next-generation/author-notes.md) now implements the approved twelve-ending structure. The dialogue remains open to revision.
Checked: 2026-10-11. Evidence is in the [profile](../people/ai-e017.md) and [quote bank](QUOTES.md).

## The three proposed goals

| User goal | Research-based interpretation | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Reach RSI and solve human problems | Seek useful scientific progress. Decide whether further self-improvement is justified. | Separate research output, autonomy, and delivered benefit. |
| Avoid AI ending humanity | Prevent loss of control and catastrophic misuse. | Tests, containment, access, and security must affect the available choices. |
| Avoid Chinese takeover and authoritarianism | Preserve democratic agency against foreign, domestic, and private concentrations of power. | A US strategic victory can still be a failure for freedom. |

These interpretations use the public positions linked in the profile. They do not assign hidden utility weights to the real person.

## What makes a decision costly

The character can want all three goals and still face a conflict between them.
For example, a delay can buy time for a better control test, consume cash, and postpone useful work.
Granting a government more access can support defense while reducing the company's ability to enforce a use restriction.
External review can improve confidence while exposing a failure that damages trust.

These are proposed causal mechanisms, not inevitable results.
For each branch, define the conditions under which the cost occurs.
Do not punish a choice merely because the story needs a crisis.

## Candidate state variables

| State | What it records | What it must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Research capability | Tasks completed under stated conditions | Automatic autonomy or a cure |
| Research autonomy | Work the system may perform without review | Reliable alignment |
| Control evidence | Results, uncertainty, and independent access | A universal safety percentage |
| Operational security | Access controls, containment, and incident response | Immunity to all attacks |
| Company capacity | Cash, compute, staff, contracts, and time | Personal control of the economy |
| Delivered benefit | Results validated and made available to people | A benchmark alone |
| Concentration of power | Who can direct, inspect, or challenge deployment | US leadership always means freedom |
| Reciprocal restraint | Verified commitments and remaining uncertainty | A promise equals compliance |
| Public legitimacy | Trust, consent, and the response to disclosures | Revenue equals public approval |

Keep some of these states hidden or uncertain. Show what Dario has evidence to know.
Treat weights, thresholds, and transition probabilities as design choices until a source supports them.

## Consequences before endings

Resolve an immediate conflict before showing a distant epilogue.
An ending must answer what happened to the project, the people affected, and control over the system.
Show the causal steps that made that result possible.
The year 2035 is a scenario horizon. It is not a required scene transition.

A candidate chain is: choice → implementation → observable result → response → new constraint.
An institutional response needs an actor, authority, incentive, and a path by which the actor learned what happened.
Do not make a government seize a company solely because a trust score crossed a threshold.
Do not make a model escape solely because the player chose a faster schedule.

## Intermediate event scenes

Intermediate scenes are required. They can show events directly, convey information, or mark the passage of time.
Possible forms include a silent cinematic, an environmental change, a character reaction, a news report, an evaluator's notice, a trial milestone, a government statement, or an internal status report.
Examples include a lab going dark, a patient receiving treatment, a harbor evacuation, or a nuclear missile launching or striking in a branch that has established the escalation leading to it.
The scene must carry a consequence or new information. It must not serve only as decoration.

Each item needs:

- A date or clear place in the sequence; attribution when the scene presents a report or claim.
- A specific event caused by prior events or the player's choice.
- A distinction between an allegation, a confirmed result, and an interpretation.
- A limited effect on what characters know and what they can do next.

Fictional headlines must be marked as scenario material in the data.
Do not invent a real publisher's report and cite it as historical evidence.
An internal result does not become public until someone releases or discovers it.
Conflicting reports can preserve uncertainty without making the story incoherent.
A cinematic can show the player something Dario does not know. His later dialogue and decisions must still depend on information he receives.

## Starting-point discipline

The September pacing essay is a dated public anchor.
For an October opening, also use the October 8 Cyber Mission announcement and October 9 incident report linked in the profile.
Anthropic had already announced that live internet access was disabled for all internal evaluations. Do not present that action as an unmade first decision.
The reported cases had limited impact according to Anthropic. A later major failure must be introduced as fiction, with its own causes.
The existing Hugging Face premise can connect the routes, but Dario's route must also confront Anthropic's own evidence and incentives.
Before scripting, choose the first decision he actually has authority to make.
Then fix the capability baseline, the available evidence, the time pressure, and the other actors' objectives.

Keep a separate record for established evidence, disputed accounts, author forecasts, and invented events.
The research does not support one guaranteed trajectory from an incident to RSI, war, or extinction.
