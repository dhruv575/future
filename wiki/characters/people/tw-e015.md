## Evidence

**Role.** TSMC's live executive page names Wei chairman and CEO. It dates his combined role from June 2024. The company had announced Mark Liu's planned retirement and recommended Wei as successor. [CH-TW-020](https://www.tsmc.com/english/aboutTSMC/executives) [CH-TW-034](https://pr.tsmc.com/system/files/newspdf/attachment/cdefe32d217bb625d5acfa2fbce9b1d57f9a2a9c/Chairman%20Annoucement_%28E%29_wmn.pdf)

**Public positions.** In the October 2025 earnings Q&A, Wei defended demand planning through close contact with customers and their customers. He said AI demand could remain strong even with limited access to China. He treated GPUs and custom accelerators as different customer designs that could both use TSMC technology. These were business judgments, not guarantees. [CH-TW-021](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-10/6860312f04fd291d0f26b46c1234f84e6332717e/TSMC%203Q25%20Transcript.pdf)

**Authority and relationships.** His executive role supports company strategy and operations. The same call shows dependence on customer requirements and packaging partners. It also shows limits on disclosure: he declined to publish some demand estimates or discuss a competitor that was also a customer. [CH-TW-020](https://www.tsmc.com/english/aboutTSMC/executives) [CH-TW-021](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-10/6860312f04fd291d0f26b46c1234f84e6332717e/TSMC%203Q25%20Transcript.pdf)

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: high for public business Q&A; untested for private crisis dialogue. Wei answers the named questioner, corrects the premise, gives a short practical explanation, and sometimes stops with a firm boundary. Technical specifics sit beside brief, dry responses. Preserve that economy. Do not copy transcript errors or spell an accent. [CH-TW-021](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-10/6860312f04fd291d0f26b46c1234f84e6332717e/TSMC%203Q25%20Transcript.pdf)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, give him a deliverable commitment from the buyer: a qualified design, payment, a volume range or a deadline. A request for more chips can fail because the requested package, power system or production window is wrong. Let him ask which constraint the buyer expects him to move.

**Resistance.** Sam can want capacity now while avoiding a long purchase obligation. A government can ask for preferred supply while refusing to absorb its cost. Wei can offer an alternative schedule or narrower allocation. This creates conflict without making him obstructive by default.

**Scene entry.** A call belongs at his level when it changes a large investment, a critical customer relationship or TSMC's ability to operate. Put operations, finance and legal staff behind the answer. He need not explain every equipment detail himself.

**Tension to preserve.** Serving rival customers requires confidentiality. Overseas expansion may support customers while placing demands on staff and capital. A fictional emergency can test whether a public delivery promise remains supportable. Neither nationality nor a government request should erase the firm's own obligations.

**Avoid.** Do not make him a philosopher who narrates the world economy in every line. Do not let him create capacity overnight, guarantee shipping through a blockade, or speak for Taiwan's government. If he refuses a request, show the constraint and an actionable alternative.

**Possible scene test.** The buyer has an urgent reservation request. Wei has one viable slot but needs a firm commitment today. The player must choose which promise to make. This is a writing premise, not a report of an actual conversation.
