## Evidence

**Role and setting.** Hoover's interview, recorded on 7 May 2026, presents Luckey as Anduril's founder and the earlier founder of Oculus. An a16z episode page separately identifies these founder roles. Neither source makes him Anduril's CEO; Brian Schimpf has that role in the reviewed record. [CH-RD-039](https://www.hoover.org/plus/research/palmer-luckey-wants-america-win), [CH-RD-040](https://a16z.com/podcast/palmer-luckey-on-hardware-building-and-the-next-frontiers-of-innovation/)

**Public argument.** In the Hoover interview, Luckey argues that the United States should equip willing partners to defend themselves. He links that position to delivery, inventory, and industrial capacity. He also criticizes procurement incentives that can reward higher costs or delay. These are his public claims, not a neutral audit of every contract type. [CH-RD-039](https://www.hoover.org/plus/research/palmer-luckey-wants-america-win)

**Origins and relationships.** He describes his own route from virtual-reality work to defense technology. That account helps explain his public framing but does not prove every causal claim about the industry. The a16z page places another conversation in a founder and investor setting. Such settings can reward a confident company story. [CH-RD-039](https://www.hoover.org/plus/research/palmer-luckey-wants-america-win), [CH-RD-040](https://a16z.com/podcast/palmer-luckey-on-hardware-building-and-the-next-frontiers-of-innovation/)

**Authority limits.** A founder can influence products, investment, and public debate. The sources do not establish unilateral power over Anduril, US procurement, allied policy, or military operations. Any specific authority in a fictional scene must be stated.

## Writer inference

**Public voice.** The Hoover transcript uses blunt analogies, examples about how products are bought, and direct challenges to a premise. He sometimes grants that a critic could reasonably disagree before returning to his practical argument. Use concrete comparisons rather than making him a permanent provocation. [CH-RD-039](https://www.hoover.org/plus/research/palmer-luckey-wants-america-win)

**Plausible scene entry.** A product review, manufacturing discussion, or meeting about a partner's order fits the record. A meeting with a model developer needs a defined reason: integration, evaluation, support, or a contractual constraint.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version could want approval to build a production line before demand is guaranteed. The obstacle could be financing, a customer specification, or a supplier's conditions. His immediate goal should be something a builder can act on.

**Useful conflict.** Give him an argument that speed itself can reduce danger, then make someone identify the failure that speed could introduce. Let him answer with a specific proposed tradeoff. The scene should not assume that either side has already won.

**Avoid.** Do not confuse the founder with the CEO or make him issue battlefield orders. Do not make criticism of bureaucracy a lack of concern about whether a system works. Do not use distinctive clothing as a substitute for character.

**Unknowns.** Private political bargaining, confidential product limits, personal crisis behavior, and precise board powers are not established. The second episode's audio was not reviewed, so no speech traits are inferred from it. Future affiliations and private conversations require explicit fictional framing or new evidence.
