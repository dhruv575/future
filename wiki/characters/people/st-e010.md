## Evidence

**Role at the source date.** An August 2018 interview introduces Grace as leading AI Impacts. She describes research that breaks broad questions about AI's future into smaller questions about evidence and trends. This is historical role evidence. It does not, by itself, establish her title in 2026. [CH-RD-021](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/katja-grace-forecasting-technology/)

**Public reasoning.** In that discussion, she treats expert forecasts as imperfect evidence and examines whether technological progress is usually continuous. She acknowledges uncertainty and sometimes declines a tidy retrospective explanation of her own choices. The useful evidence is how she examines a claim, not a fixed set of predictions to carry into every future scene. [CH-RD-021](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/katja-grace-forecasting-technology/)

**Research.** Grace is a coauthor of the January 2024 survey of thousands of AI researchers. Its abstract reports a wide range of expectations about AI capabilities and consequences. Only the abstract was reviewed here. Survey aggregation is not a calibrated estimate of war risk, and a response distribution is not the lead author's personal belief. [CH-RD-022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)

**Tension.** Research can be useful for decisions even when it cannot supply an exact arrival date. This is different from treating uncertainty as a reason to abandon forecasting. It also differs from treating an impressive respondent count as proof that the median outcome will occur.

## Writer inference

**Public voice.** She qualifies scope, separates questions, admits uncertainty, and uses ordinary humor. Do not copy verbal fillers as a gimmick. Confidence is medium for this historical public setting. [CH-RD-021](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/katja-grace-forecasting-technology/)

**Plausible scene entry.** She could be invited to review a survey headline that is shaping a policy decision. The scene needs a clear distinction between what respondents were asked and what decision-makers now claim the result means.

**Possible wants and resistance.** Give her a practical objective, such as preserving the wording of a forecast question in a public brief. The opposing pressure could be a need for a single date or number that is easy to communicate. That disagreement can expose how much the decision depends on a definition.

**Useful conflict.** Let another character ask what can still be concluded. The fictional researcher should provide a useful bounded answer, not only caveats. The scene should leave the player with a clearer choice.

**Avoid.** Do not make her speak for all AI researchers. Do not equate a survey response about extinction with a forecast of US–China war. Do not infer a present policy demand from a 2018 discussion.

**Unknowns.** A current first-person interview is needed before asserting how her views have changed. This brief does not establish private relationships, emotional reactions in a crisis, or a future position in government. Those details must remain absent or be clearly fictional.
