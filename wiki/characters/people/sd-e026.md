## Evidence

The White House identifies Vance as vice president. The undated page was checked on 2026-10-11. [CH-SD-007](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/jd-vance/)

In Paris in February 2025, he argued for AI growth, US technology leadership, worker benefits, and less restrictive regulation. He questioned whether incumbent companies use safety rules to protect their position. He also supported blocking technology theft and hostile uses. [CH-SD-005](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-the-vice-president-the-artificial-intelligence-action-summit-paris-france)

He addressed European leaders as potential partners, while challenging their regulation of US firms. He connected AI to physical infrastructure and workers. His position was not simply that software progress is always beneficial. It included an industrial and national argument. [CH-SD-005](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-the-vice-president-the-artificial-intelligence-action-summit-paris-france)

A May 2026 pool report records a different emphasis. At the Air Force Academy, he expressed concern about AI warfare. He said humans should make life-and-death decisions. The report contains selected remarks, not the full speech. [CH-SD-006](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/vice-presidential-pool-reports-may-28-2026)

CRS places nuclear authorization with the president. Do not give a vice president an independent launch decision while the president retains authority. [CH-SD-004](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10521)

## Writer inference

**Voice analysis:** The Paris speech opens with a personal joke, then gives a numbered argument. It uses contrasts and rhetorical questions. He challenges an opponent's premise and connects a technical claim to workers or national independence. The cadence is explanatory but combative. [CH-SD-005](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-the-vice-president-the-artificial-intelligence-action-summit-paris-france)

Use clear claims followed by reasons. Let another speaker dispute the reason. Avoid a long policy lecture. One pointed question is more useful than several slogans.

**FICTION DESIGN ANALYSIS — scene uses:**

- Give him a safety proposal that may also entrench its sponsor.
- Let him request a case for the proposal's effect on small firms or workers.
- Put a useful military tool beside a disputed delegation of lethal judgment.
- Have him seek a narrower rule instead of accepting a choice between total freedom and total prohibition.
- Make his authority depend on an explicit presidential assignment in the scene.

An invented objective could be to secure growth without handing control to a small group of vendors. A plausible resistance is a request that asks the government to trust a firm's private assessment. A plausible question concerns who remains responsible when an automated recommendation is wrong.

**Tension to preserve:** Pro-development rhetoric in 2025 and human control in 2026 concern different uses. They need not be treated as a reversal. A character can reject broad restrictions and still resist a specific military delegation. [CH-SD-005](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-the-vice-president-the-artificial-intelligence-action-summit-paris-france) [CH-SD-006](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/vice-presidential-pool-reports-may-28-2026)
