# Jensen Huang

Entity: `TW-E020`; aliases: none. Research checked: 2026-10-11.

## Evidence

**Role.** NVIDIA's live profile identifies Huang as co-founder, president, CEO and board member. It dates his leadership to the company's founding in 1993. This is strong evidence of company position, not unlimited control over the supply chain. [CH-TW-027](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/about-nvidia/board-of-directors/jensen-huang/)

**Public positions.** The company's account of his 2025 COMPUTEX keynote presents AI as infrastructure. His explanation turns data centers into production facilities, with energy as an input and tokens as an output. He puts partners and complete systems at the center of that case. This is a commercial thesis, not an established forecast of demand. [CH-TW-028](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/computex-2025-jensen-huang/)

A July 2025 NVIDIA readout describes Huang promoting US AI leadership and domestic manufacturing in Washington, while discussing productivity and opportunity in Beijing. It also described plans to resume H20 sales. These were dated company claims; they do not establish a permanent export permission. [CH-TW-029](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-ceo-promotes-ai-in-dc-and-china/)

**Relationships and limits.** His position gives him influence over NVIDIA strategy and major commercial commitments. The record shows engagement with policymakers in both countries. It does not make him the person who sets US controls or decides China's response. [CH-TW-027](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/about-nvidia/board-of-directors/jensen-huang/) [CH-TW-029](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-ceo-promotes-ai-in-dc-and-china/)

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium. The keynote excerpts use physical analogies to make a large technical transition seem concrete and inevitable. They scale from a machine to an industry, then return to products and partners. Give him a clear thesis and something tangible to point at. Reserve the full keynote register for a public setting. [CH-TW-028](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/computex-2025-jensen-huang/)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can seek a credible deployment plan that keeps customers buying and partners building. He can argue that a bottleneck is solvable if the buyer commits enough money, engineering work and time. He may need the buyer to accept a whole system rather than one component.

**Resistance.** Sam can want performance with flexibility. A government can want a clean geographic boundary that does not match a commercial network. A foundry can demand certainty while NVIDIA's buyers change their forecasts. Put one of these conflicts on the table.

**Scene entry.** A product launch, customer capacity discussion or formal policy meeting fits. A battlefield incident needs a link to NVIDIA systems, personnel or export exposure before he becomes a principal.

**Tension to preserve.** Supporting US leadership and seeking global customers can pull in different directions. Public confidence in demand can coexist with a hard negotiation over who carries investment risk. Do not assume that optimistic rhetoric removes either tension.

**Avoid.** Do not make him Sam's obedient source of unlimited GPUs. Do not give him control of TSMC, power generation or shipping. Do not reduce him to sales slogans or an accent. Let him decline a request, ask for proof, or name a cost.

**Possible scene test.** Huang offers a workable system on a later date. Sam needs a headline now. The question is which commitment each side is prepared to sign.

## Gaps

- Keynote excerpts and company readouts do not establish private negotiation behavior.
- A full unscripted interview is still needed for high-confidence voice.
- Current export licenses and capacity promises require separate checks.
