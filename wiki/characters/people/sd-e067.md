# Alex Karp

This brief separates public evidence from writing guidance. Future roles, private scenes, and dialogue are fiction unless separately sourced.

## Evidence

**Role.** CNBC identifies Karp as Palantir's cofounder and CEO in its September 2026 interview excerpts. This verifies the public role at that date. It does not give him authority over a customer's military or political decisions. [CH-RD-037](https://pressroom.versantmedia.com/cnbc/press-releases/cnbc-exclusive-excerpts-palantir-co-founder-ceo-alex-karp-speaks-cnbcs-squawk)

**A durable public frame.** His 2020 CEO letter in Palantir's public filing argues that important institutions need effective software. It defends work with defense and intelligence customers and questions whether technical expertise should also confer authority over society's moral choices. This is prepared corporate advocacy. It should not be mistaken for an independent assessment of Palantir's conduct. [CH-RD-038](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000119312520230013/d904406ds1.htm)

**A later qualification.** In the 2026 CNBC excerpts, Karp combines a US–China competitive frame with concern about unequal gains from AI and accountability for harm. He praises Dario Amodei while criticizing aspects of the wider industry's approach to data and liability. This does not support writing him as hostile to every safety argument or every rival executive. [CH-RD-037](https://pressroom.versantmedia.com/cnbc/press-releases/cnbc-exclusive-excerpts-palantir-co-founder-ceo-alex-karp-speaks-cnbcs-squawk)

**Relationships and limits.** Praise does not establish a private alliance. Reported customer concerns do not prove misconduct. The source is excerpted and unofficial; its capital letters do not establish shouting.

## Writer inference

**Public voice.** In these samples, Karp challenges the frame of a question and turns business issues into arguments about responsibility, institutions, and political order. The interview includes repetition and sharp contrasts. The older letter is more structured. Use this range, not a stream of slogans. [CH-RD-037](https://pressroom.versantmedia.com/cnbc/press-releases/cnbc-exclusive-excerpts-palantir-co-founder-ceo-alex-karp-speaks-cnbcs-squawk), [CH-RD-038](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000119312520230013/d904406ds1.htm)

**Plausible scene entry.** He can appear in a partnership negotiation, customer meeting, or public exchange. Establish why Palantir is needed for the specific problem. He should not arrive merely because the topic is military AI.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version might seek a deployment decision with a clear owner and a usable product. Resistance can come from a model supplier that wants control over access, a customer worried about dependence, or a lawyer asking who accepts liability.

**Useful conflict.** Let him force another character to state who benefits and who carries the cost. Then let that character challenge whether Palantir's proposed answer serves the same interests. Both should have a concrete stake.

**Avoid.** Do not turn ideological language into secret intent to cause war. Do not erase business incentives or present them as proof that his stated beliefs are false. Do not write every interaction as an angry public performance.

**Unknowns.** Private relationships, confidential contracts, and internal release decisions are not established here. A future state appointment, privileged operational access, or specific wrongdoing must not be inferred from his public role. Future scenes should identify their fictional additions.
