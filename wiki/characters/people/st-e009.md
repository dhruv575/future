## Evidence

**Role and authority.** Carnegie's biography identifies Sheehan as a senior fellow whose work covers technology with a focus on China. The role supports policy analysis and convening. It does not make him an envoy, regulator, or representative of the Chinese government. [CH-RD-018](https://carnegieendowment.org/people/matt-sheehan)

**Stated approach.** In a 2021 first-person introduction, he describes revising assumptions learned around Silicon Valley after studying China's technology development. He argues that failing to take Chinese AI governance seriously can lead to poor US policy. This is an account of his intellectual approach, not an endorsement of every Chinese policy. [CH-RD-019](https://carnegieendowment.org/posts/2021/10/matt-sheehan-new-scholar-spotlight)

**Later proposal.** In July 2026, he proposes limited channels that improve safety work on both sides. The aim is useful information exchange and stronger domestic capabilities. Binding agreements are a possible benefit rather than the initial requirement. He acknowledges that this modest approach might be insufficient and may need to change. [CH-RD-020](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2026/07/ai-safety-parallel-us-china)

**Tension.** His proposal accepts rivalry and political limits while looking for practical work that can proceed. It is not a claim that competition has ended, that trust already exists, or that an ambitious treaty would be easy. Its value and sufficiency remain matters for assessment.

## Writer inference

**Voice confidence: low for speech.** The written samples challenge a familiar assumption, explain why it failed, and propose a more bounded alternative. They are good evidence for argument structure. They are not enough to reproduce conversational rhythm. Keep dialogue natural and spare. [CH-RD-019](https://carnegieendowment.org/posts/2021/10/matt-sheehan-new-scholar-spotlight)

**Plausible scene entry.** Use an outside briefing before a technical exchange, or a workshop that tests what each side could share. Establish who has asked for his advice and who actually has authority to approve the exchange.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version could want one limited information channel to remain open during a broader dispute. One side may fear leakage; the other may fear being seen as conceding. A concrete result, such as agreeing on the scope of a joint discussion, is more credible than asking him to secure peace.

**Useful conflict.** Let a participant argue that a modest channel is inadequate. He can defend what it makes possible while conceding its limits. The 2026 essay supports this form of tension. [CH-RD-020](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2026/07/ai-safety-parallel-us-china)

**Avoid.** Do not portray him as a simple advocate for accommodation, or as proof that all researchers in China share one position. Do not have him translate language ability into knowledge of secret intent. Do not make an informal discussion equivalent to a state agreement.

**Unknowns.** This record does not verify the participants in any private dialogue, what classified information he could access, or a personal relationship with the game's leaders. Substantial speaking scenes need a direct interview. Future roles and access require separate evidence or explicit fiction.
