## Evidence

**Role and expertise.** RAND's biography identifies Mueller as a senior political scientist and professor of policy analysis. His work includes coercion, deterrence, escalation, and wargaming. It records earlier teaching at the US Air Force's School of Advanced Air and Space Studies. This is a research career, not current authority to direct forces. [CH-RD-033](https://www.rand.org/about/people/m/mueller_karl_p.html)

**Research links.** He coauthored Breakwater with Abby Doll. That game's overview explores instability during AI competition and reports early hypotheses. This is a documented collaboration and a research method, not a real conflict forecast. [CH-RD-034](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4703-1.html)

**Policy argument.** With David Frelinger, he proposes an AGI Rideout strategy. Its public summary emphasizes resilience and avoiding strategic disaster during an uncertain transition. It does not require assuming that either racing faster or slowing development is always the correct response. [CH-RD-035](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4347-1.html)

**Individual explanation.** In a September 2026 RAND podcast, Mueller describes a transition that governments cannot fully control. He uses a river metaphor to explain why surviving the process and retaining options matter. He distinguishes his use of “rideout” from the term's narrower nuclear history. His comments on China are analysis, not access to its plans. [CH-RD-036](https://www.rand.org/pubs/podcasts/policy-minded/2026/agi-rideout-managing-risk-on-the-road-to-advanced-ai.html)

## Writer inference

**Public voice.** The interview clarifies definitions, explains historical analogies, and corrects an analogy before it becomes misleading. He argues for practical options under uncertainty. Confidence is medium for this public explanatory mode. Do not copy the river metaphor into every scene. [CH-RD-036](https://www.rand.org/pubs/podcasts/policy-minded/2026/agi-rideout-managing-risk-on-the-road-to-advanced-ai.html)

**Plausible scene entry.** Use an invited review of a strategy that assumes a short, decisive race. His role can be to identify what the plan overlooks if the transition is longer or less predictable.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version might seek funding for resilience that does not produce a visible lead on a benchmark. The resistance is an institution that rewards progress against a single competitive target. This creates a concrete choice about resources and preparedness.

**Useful conflict.** Have him ask what options remain if the favored forecast is wrong. A decision-maker can then choose whether to accept a slower visible gain in exchange for more freedom to act later.

**Avoid.** Do not portray resilience as passivity, surrender, or a guarantee of peace. Do not make his research a reason to freeze all AI work. Do not turn a coauthored game into a personal desire for preventive conflict.

**Unknowns.** The record does not establish his private relationships with executives, access to current operations, or a future official role. A fictional character can disagree with his argument. The story should show the disagreement through a decision, rather than appointing him the voice of objective truth.
