## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium for a formal company discussion, low for unscripted conflict. The annual-report answers move from an industry condition to a company response, then to a practical benefit. Use that sequence without copying promotional phrasing. Do not mistake polished company editing for his exact private speech. [CH-TW-025](https://investor.asml.com/annual-reports/2025/ceo-message)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can seek a stable planning assumption that lets ASML commit suppliers and field support. A customer promise, a license decision and an installation schedule are separate things. A good scene makes someone admit which of those they can actually provide.

**Resistance.** A government can ask the firm to preserve technology leadership while restricting a market. A customer can demand an accelerated delivery despite missing site readiness. An AI lab can describe demand in compute terms while ASML needs a foundry-level order.

**Scene entry.** Use an industry meeting, a discussion with a major chip manufacturer or a government consultation on a large policy change. Sam would usually enter through his chip supplier or an organized summit. Explain a direct emergency meeting if the story needs one.

**Tension to preserve.** An equipment supplier needs long planning cycles while public policy can change quickly. Compliance can protect the firm while disappointing customers. Supporting domestic industry can also require a global supplier base. Show a specific blocked service or delayed installation rather than a lecture about dependencies.

**Avoid.** Do not give him personal authority to revoke Dutch export law. Do not treat possession of a machine as sufficient for a functioning fab. Do not make him a generic European spokesman who agrees with every EU official. A business request to officials is not a state order.

**Possible scene test.** A customer wants an assurance for investors. Fouquet can offer a dated engineering milestone, but cannot guarantee a pending license. The player must decide what to say publicly.
