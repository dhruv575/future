# Lee Jae Myung

Entity: `SD-E038`; aliases: none. Research checked: 2026-10-11.

## Evidence

**Role.** President of the Republic of Korea on the official profile reviewed 11 October 2026. The July and August speeches provide dated corroboration. [CH-TW-041](https://en.president.go.kr/president) [CH-TW-042](https://www.president.go.kr/speeches/hHXHeToZ) [CH-TW-043](https://en.president.go.kr/president/statements-remarks/MK8xT9SG)

**Public positions.** In the July San Francisco AI declaration, Lee linked semiconductor production, compute, energy, data and talent. He presented Korea as a potential supply and deployment partner. He also discussed broad access to AI's gains. This was an industrial and social-policy vision, not proof that the promised capacity had been built. The summary here is translated from Korean. [CH-TW-042](https://www.president.go.kr/speeches/hHXHeToZ)

In his August Liberation Day address, he proposed mechanisms to reduce tensions and develop peaceful coexistence on the peninsula. He rejected unification by absorption and linked peace to economic prospects. He also described cooperation with Japan. The English page labels its text an unofficial translation. [CH-TW-043](https://en.president.go.kr/president/statements-remarks/MK8xT9SG)

**Authority and relationships.** These speeches establish a presidential agenda that joins growth and security. They do not turn private semiconductor firms into government departments. Nor do they establish that North Korea accepted his proposals.

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium for public argument structure; low for original-language conversational detail. The speeches use national history, a future promise and numbered steps. They bring the policy back to ordinary people's opportunities. Use concrete economic and social stakes in a scene. Do not merely have him repeat another leader's security language. [CH-TW-042](https://www.president.go.kr/speeches/hHXHeToZ) [CH-TW-043](https://en.president.go.kr/president/statements-remarks/MK8xT9SG)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can seek a technology partnership that improves Korean capacity while limiting exposure to a conflict elsewhere. He can ask what remains in Korea after a foreign company's investment, and how an agreement affects households, workers and domestic firms.

**Resistance.** An ally may want restrictions that carry large commercial costs. A company may promise growth but require public power, land or finance. A security crisis elsewhere may demand resources while peninsula risks remain active.

**Scene entry.** A summit, a national industrial agreement or a security meeting about a spillover risk fits. A direct call from Sam needs a project, prior meeting or official channel that explains the access.

**Tension to preserve.** Korea can seek a central place in global AI production and still need protection from supply disruption. Outreach to the North can coexist with preparation for failure. A fictional decision should show what the government gives up to preserve both aims.

**Avoid.** Do not make him the automatic representative of Samsung or SK hynix. Do not imply that a peace proposal proves trust in every counterpart. Do not infer hostility to the US from a desire to avoid peninsula escalation. No reviewed source settles how he would respond to a rogue foreign model attacking Korean infrastructure.

**Possible scene test.** A foreign lab asks Korea to host a recovery cluster during a Taiwan disruption. The offer brings investment but also an external security risk. Lee needs clear terms on control, continuity and public cost before he can decide.
