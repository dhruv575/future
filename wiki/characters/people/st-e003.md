## Evidence

**Role and background.** King's College London identifies Payne as a professor of strategy. His research includes political psychology, strategic studies, and AI. His books connect human psychology and the history of conflict with questions about machine decision-making. The biography also records advice to governments and parliamentary appearances. These establish professional experience, not an operational command role. [CH-RD-005](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/people/payne-dr-kenneth)

**Stated role.** In a dated 2023 university profile, Payne describes part of his work as bringing different professional communities together. The article discusses his work with policymakers, military practitioners, and researchers. The useful distinction is between explaining technology in historical context and deciding how a government will use it. Only the article text was reviewed; the embedded video was not. [CH-RD-006](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/spotlight/helping-the-world-understand-the-implications-of-artificial-intelligence)

**Research position.** His February 2026 preprint studies frontier models in simulated nuclear crises. It reports model behavior that the author interprets as strategic reasoning, including deception and responses to opponents. These are claims about a designed experiment. They do not establish conscious intent, reliable strategic judgment, or a real-world nuclear-use probability. The sample and game conditions limit what can be inferred. [CH-RD-007](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14740v1)

**Tension.** This research treats model conduct as strategically interesting, while its setting is artificial. A writer must preserve both facts. Describing sophisticated behavior is not the same as endorsing autonomous military decisions.

## Writer inference

**Voice confidence: low.** This review supports his subject matter and explanatory approach more strongly than his conversational rhythm. The short attributed statements in the university profile are too limited for an imitation. Use clear explanations that connect a present decision to a relevant historical or psychological mechanism. Treat this as role-based guidance. [CH-RD-006](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/spotlight/helping-the-world-understand-the-implications-of-artificial-intelligence)

**Plausible scene entry.** He could be invited to brief a policy team about an experiment that has generated alarming headlines. Establish who invited him and what decision the briefing is meant to inform. He does not need to appear in a live launch room to be useful.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version might want officials to investigate an unexpected model strategy before incorporating that model into planning. The competing pressure is a desire to announce either success or danger from a striking result. Make the disagreement turn on a specific inference.

**Scene use.** Ask him to explain what a model chose, what options it had, and why a human audience might misread that choice. Another character can challenge whether the setup resembles a real command system. Let that challenge matter.

**Avoid.** Do not reduce him to the claim that “AI will launch nuclear weapons.” Do not transfer laboratory outcome percentages into geopolitical forecasts. Do not give him access to private military intent.

**Unknowns.** This brief does not establish how he speaks during a private dispute, his personal confidence in every reported finding, or a later public appointment. Recheck those points before writing them as fact.
