## Evidence

**Role.** Australia's live official profile names Albanese as prime minister. The detailed security material in this brief is from 2023. A live biography does not make every older statement a current operational policy. [CH-TW-044](https://www.pm.gov.au/about-prime-minister)

**Public positions.** In his Shangri-La address, Albanese argued that direct US-China communication reduces the risk of misunderstanding becoming action. He warned that a Taiwan Strait conflict would have global effects. He also described Australian engagement with China as compatible with continuing disagreement. [CH-TW-045](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/iiss-shangri-la-dialogue)

In the accompanying Q&A, he presented Australia as a participant in several partnerships and forums, with a role in bringing states together. The answer combined cooperation with maintaining Australia's values. These are public positions rather than proof that talks will succeed. [CH-TW-046](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/qa-iiss-shangri-la-dialogue-2023)

**Relationships and authority.** The material supports a prime minister's role in national diplomacy and policy. It does not make Australia a passive extension of the United States or give a prime minister control of another ally's forces. [CH-TW-044](https://www.pm.gov.au/about-prime-minister) [CH-TW-045](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/iiss-shangri-la-dialogue)

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: high for the sampled public English remarks; private dialogue remains an inference. His Q&A uses a direct acknowledgment, a practical example and a restated principle. He can explain disagreement without presenting it as the end of a relationship. Keep the concrete example and shorten the repetition in a game scene. [CH-TW-046](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/qa-iiss-shangri-la-dialogue-2023)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can seek an action that preserves Australian agency, regional stability and a useful channel to both major powers. That action may be a joint statement, an incident contact or a limited offer of assistance.

**Resistance.** A counterpart may demand a public commitment before providing evidence. A company may want Australia to host a service that brings security obligations. Domestic ministers may disagree about the economic and defense costs.

**Scene entry.** Bring him into a leaders' call, a cabinet-level decision or a major national investment. Routine technical coordination should pass through officials. If a company's mistake becomes a diplomatic incident, show that transition before he appears.

**Tension to preserve.** Dialogue is useful even when it does not change a counterpart's conduct. Security partnerships create choices rather than eliminating them. Let the player see what Australia can offer, which conditions it seeks and what would make it refuse.

**Avoid.** Do not write him as an all-purpose mediator with instant access to a settlement. Do not use his preference for communication as proof that he opposes every deterrent measure. Do not substitute friendly remarks for a specific Australian commitment.

**Possible scene test.** Two partners want Australia to endorse competing accounts of an AI incident. Albanese has enough information to propose an investigation but not enough to endorse attribution. The costs of that position should appear in subsequent relations, rather than an invisible virtue score.
