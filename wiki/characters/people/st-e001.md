## Evidence

**Role and authority.** Hoover's live biography identifies Schneider as the Hargrove Hoover Fellow and director of its Wargaming and Crisis Simulation Initiative. Her work combines national security, technology, and political psychology. It records prior Air Force service, academic work, and policy advice. These are distinct roles. An academic presentation does not carry military command authority. [CH-RD-001](https://www.hoover.org/profiles/jacquelyn-schneider)

**Public positions.** In an institutional interview, she explains why repeated games and preserved player records matter. A polished after-action summary can hide how conclusions were reached. She also distinguishes a precise research definition of a wargame from the wider range of historical exercises. Her concern is how evidence was produced, not just whether a scenario feels plausible. [CH-RD-002](https://www.hoover.org/research/wargamers-search-answers)

**Research and relationships.** She coauthored the 2024 human–model comparison with Max Lamparth and other researchers. The study compares behavior within a specified simulation. It does not establish that a model can stand in for a real national leader. This is a documented working link to Lamparth, not proof of agreement on every policy. [CH-RD-003](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03407v4)

**Tension.** Her interview gives examples in which player confidence, expertise, and reporting practices complicate intuitive lessons. She describes AI oversight preferences as game findings. Do not turn them into an assertion that every official trusts, or distrusts, AI in the same way. [CH-RD-002](https://www.hoover.org/research/wargamers-search-answers)

## Writer inference

**Public voice.** The reviewed interview moves from a definition to a concrete example, then to a qualification. She can challenge the question before answering it. Occasional dry humor makes the technical discussion conversational. Use this as a light guide, not a verbal imitation. Confidence is medium because this brief relies on one main interview. [CH-RD-002](https://www.hoover.org/research/wargamers-search-answers)

**Plausible scene entry.** Invite her to review a crisis exercise or explain a surprising result. A staff member can have arranged the session. Her reason to stay in the scene is a contested conclusion: a sponsor wants to call the exercise proof that its plan works.

**Possible wants and resistance.** Give her a bounded objective, such as obtaining the raw decision record before a briefing is published. The obstacle could be a sponsor's deadline or an incomplete transcript. The negotiation should concern what can responsibly be claimed, rather than a generic request to “be safe.”

**Avoid.** Do not use her as a narrator who knows how China will react. Do not make one unusual game outcome her personal prediction. Do not assume that prior service gives her access to a current classified operation.

**Unknowns.** This record does not establish her private relationships with executives, her conduct under real crisis pressure, or a position she would hold in 2035. Any future appointment or private meeting must be explicit fiction.
