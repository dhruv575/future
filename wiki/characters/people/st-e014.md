## Evidence

**Role and methods.** RAND's live biography identifies Sytsma as an economist and professor of policy analysis. His work includes AI strategy, economic competition, supply-chain risk, game theory, and decisions under deep uncertainty. These are tools for analyzing choices. They are not authority to make those choices for a government. [CH-RD-029](https://www.rand.org/about/people/s/sytsma_tobias.html)

**Research with Alvin Moon.** Their September 2026 report models conditions for mutual restraint. Its public summary emphasizes domestic oversight and the ability to detect noncompliance. It also explains why incentives can change when actors believe AGI is near. This is a stylized model, not an observed frequency of cheating or war. [CH-RD-031](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA5163-1.html)

**Individual public reasoning.** In RAND's June 2025 podcast on work, Sytsma distinguishes exposure to AI from harm to workers. He discusses possible productivity gains while identifying uncertainty about longer-term effects. The distinction matters: a technical term does not automatically imply a policy conclusion. [CH-RD-048](https://www.rand.org/pubs/podcasts/policy-minded/2025/how-will-AI-reshape-work.html)

**Relationships and limits.** Moon is a documented research collaborator. Their paper does not establish personal agreement on every intervention. RAND affiliation is also not evidence of classified access or a mandate to negotiate with a rival state.

## Writer inference

**Public voice.** The podcast defines a term, gives an accessible explanation, and separates an observed result from an open question. Sytsma acknowledges what he cannot yet answer. Use that pattern rather than a generic economist stereotype. Confidence is medium for a public explanation, lower for a crisis scene. [CH-RD-048](https://www.rand.org/pubs/podcasts/policy-minded/2025/how-will-AI-reshape-work.html)

**Plausible scene entry.** Invite him to test whether a proposed agreement changes incentives as its sponsors claim. Give him a narrow problem: the conditions under which an inspection scheme might help two sides remain restrained.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version could seek agreement on the assumptions before presenting a model result. Another participant wants one probability or a simple assurance. He can offer a conditional answer that still changes a decision.

**Useful conflict.** A model may show that a proposal works only if one institution can do something it currently cannot do. The scene should then move to whether that institution can be built, funded, or trusted. This is a story-design inference, not a forecast of his behavior.

**Avoid.** Do not turn mathematical analysis into moral indifference. Do not make model variables reveal real leaders' private preferences. Do not have him state that a model proves cooperation will succeed.

**Unknowns.** This record does not establish private policy preferences, negotiating style, or personal links to the game's executives. The professional voice sample comes from a different topic. A later public appointment or confidential advisory role requires separate evidence.
