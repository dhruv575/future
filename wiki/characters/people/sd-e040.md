## Evidence

**Role.** Deputy prime minister and defence minister in the April 2026 official strategy account. The 2025 interview pages identify the same positions. [CH-TW-047](https://www.defence.gov.au/news-events/news/2026-04-17/preparing-australia-future-strategic-challenges) [CH-TW-048](https://www.minister.defence.gov.au/transcripts/2025-06-16/defending-australia-2025-summit-qa) [CH-TW-049](https://www.minister.defence.gov.au/transcripts/2025-06-01/doorstop-interview-singapore)

**Public positions.** In 2026, Marles linked Australia's security to sea routes beyond its coastline and emphasized deterrence, self-reliance and partnerships. The department presented the strategy as a continuation with stronger emphasis, not a complete reversal. Announced investment is not the same as delivered capability. [CH-TW-047](https://www.defence.gov.au/news-events/news/2026-04-17/preparing-australia-future-strategic-challenges)

In a 2025 Q&A, he disputed an interviewer's framing of defense spending. He defended present procurement while arguing that spending and capability take time to expand. In Singapore he emphasized operational cooperation and described AUKUS as technology cooperation, including nuclear-powered submarines. His progress claims remain attributed claims. [CH-TW-048](https://www.minister.defence.gov.au/transcripts/2025-06-16/defending-australia-2025-summit-qa) [CH-TW-049](https://www.minister.defence.gov.au/transcripts/2025-06-01/doorstop-interview-singapore)

**Relationships and limits.** The Singapore record names US, Japanese and Philippine counterparts. Cooperation has practical work behind it. It is not a single shared chain of command. [CH-TW-049](https://www.minister.defence.gov.au/transcripts/2025-06-01/doorstop-interview-singapore)

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: high for public answers under challenge. The Q&A shows a habit of unpacking the premise before answering. He adds qualifications, disputes an omitted fact and brings the answer back to what has been done. Use that friction in dialogue. He need not agree with the question's frame. [CH-TW-048](https://www.minister.defence.gov.au/transcripts/2025-06-16/defending-australia-2025-summit-qa)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can need a capability that works within a schedule and can be sustained by Australia. An AI tool's demonstration is not enough. He can ask who maintains it, how it fits other systems and what happens if access ends.

**Resistance.** Industry may promise speed without operational support. An ally may offer integration while retaining important control. A budget choice may improve one area and delay another. Let him ask what must stop to fund the new plan.

**Scene entry.** A defense capability review, alliance ministerial meeting or serious operational incident fits. A prime minister's broad commitment can lead into his scene, where officials must make it workable.

**Tension to preserve.** Alliance cooperation and national control are not identical. A faster procurement route can increase dependence. A slower domestic route can leave a capability gap. Give him an argument for each rather than treating self-reliance as a free bonus.

**Avoid.** Do not turn every answer into cautious diplomatic reassurance. He can push back on an interviewer or supplier. Do not allow an announced budget to conjure deployable systems. Do not assume a technology-cooperation agreement automatically commits Australian forces to a particular war.

**Possible scene test.** A vendor offers a rapid pilot during a crisis. Marles can approve a limited use only if the operational commander has a fallback and the contract preserves access to essential support. The precise conditions are a fictional design proposal, not a verified Australian policy.
