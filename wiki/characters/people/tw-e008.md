## Evidence

**Role.** President of Taiwan in the 10 October 2026 National Day address. The record does not fix his office through 2035. His 2024 inauguration tied his mandate to elections and the ROC constitutional system. [CH-TW-001](https://english.president.gov.tw/NEWS/6726) [CH-TW-002](https://english.president.gov.tw/News/7226)

**Public positions.** In 2024, he paired defense of self-government with offers of dialogue. His TIME answers said stability in China could make Taiwan safer. He presented continuity with Tsai Ing-wen's commitments as a reason that his position was not a provocation. These are his stated arguments, not proof that Beijing accepts them. [CH-TW-003](https://time.com/6985706/taiwan-president-lai-ching-te-interview-full-transcript/)

In 2026, he linked defense to keeping energy, transport, communications and health services working. He advocated asymmetric capability and domestic industry. The same address devoted substantial attention to families, housing and healthcare. A scene that gives him only military concerns loses a large part of this public agenda. [CH-TW-002](https://english.president.gov.tw/News/7226)

**Authority and relationships.** Constitutional offices have separate duties. The cabinet and legislature remain necessary to execute policy and approve budgets. [CH-TW-004](https://english.president.gov.tw/Page/95)

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium. The inauguration uses history, collective duty and clear political contrasts. The interview answers restate a principle, defend its continuity, then explain its intended effect. Use this argument structure in English. Do not imitate translated sentence rhythm or turn every private exchange into a ceremonial address. [CH-TW-001](https://english.president.gov.tw/NEWS/6726) [CH-TW-003](https://time.com/6985706/taiwan-president-lai-ching-te-interview-full-transcript/)

**Plausible wants.** In a fictional crisis, he can seek a concrete measure that protects Taiwan without surrendering the right to decide its future. Give him a public responsibility with a visible cost: preserving hospital power, keeping a port operating, or defending a budget before skeptical legislators.

**Resistance.** He can reject a foreign company's demand for secrecy if it leaves Taiwan responsible for consequences it cannot assess. He can also reject a dramatic public accusation if his advisers cannot support it. Neither response is predetermined by his party.

**Scene entry.** Bring him into a technology dispute after the premier or security team shows that it affects national continuity, allied commitments or a presidential statement. Routine cloud-service failures belong with staff first.

**Tension to preserve.** He may need foreign support while resisting foreign control. He may want a firm signal while fearing the costs borne by citizens. Make a foreign partner answer what Taiwan receives and what Taiwan must risk.

**Avoid.** Do not make him casually offer TSMC output as a personal asset. Do not infer a wish for war from support for self-government. A reversal of his stated position needs a visible event and argument.
