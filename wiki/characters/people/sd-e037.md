## Evidence

**Role.** President of the Philippines in the May 2024 IISS transcript. The official news index also identifies him as president in an October 2026 state-visit item. Direct access to that index failed, so the later role check is limited to its search extract. [CH-TW-039](https://www.iiss.org/globalassets/media-library---content--migration/files/shangri-la-dialogue/2024/transcripts/final/keynote-address/ferdinand-r-marcos-jr-president-philippines--as-delivered.pdf) [CH-TW-040](https://pco.gov.ph/news_releases/)

**Public positions.** At Shangri-La, Marcos defended Philippine maritime rights through international law. He rejected a view of regional states as mere objects of US-China rivalry. He supported the US alliance, recognized China's enduring regional influence and called for restraint around Taiwan. He linked the Taiwan Strait to geography and Filipinos living there. [CH-TW-039](https://www.iiss.org/globalassets/media-library---content--migration/files/shangri-la-dialogue/2024/transcripts/final/keynote-address/ferdinand-r-marcos-jr-president-philippines--as-delivered.pdf)

The same speech called for Indo-Pacific participation in discussions on lethal autonomous weapons and responsible conduct in space and cyberspace. It does not supply a detailed personal policy on LLM deployment or a specific crisis response. [CH-TW-039](https://www.iiss.org/globalassets/media-library---content--migration/files/shangri-la-dialogue/2024/transcripts/final/keynote-address/ferdinand-r-marcos-jr-president-philippines--as-delivered.pdf)

**Authority limit.** A presidential speech establishes an official position. It does not demonstrate that any requested force, budget or allied response is available. Establish national procedures separately before a scene gives him a binding military option.

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium for formal English speeches. The IISS address starts from sovereignty and law, names a specific national stake, then widens to regional responsibility. Firm assertions alternate with offers of cooperation. Keep that combination; neither generic de-escalation language nor constant threats captures it. [CH-TW-039](https://www.iiss.org/globalassets/media-library---content--migration/files/shangri-la-dialogue/2024/transcripts/final/keynote-address/ferdinand-r-marcos-jr-president-philippines--as-delivered.pdf)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can seek protection for a named Philippine interest: crews, a resupply mission, an evacuation route or maritime access. That objective should remain visible even if US and Chinese characters frame the incident in larger terms.

**Resistance.** An ally may seek access or support without committing to protect the people exposed by that choice. A company may offer sensor or model outputs that officials cannot yet verify. A neighboring government may offer talks while a confrontation continues at sea.

**Scene entry.** Use a presidential security briefing after a specific incident, a leaders' call or a decision about an allied request. Give the coast guard, armed forces and diplomatic staff their own information. The president should not receive an AI firm's confidence score as settled fact.

**Tension to preserve.** Avoiding escalation and refusing to yield a claimed right can collide in a real decision. The player may have to decide what action preserves access while keeping a route back to talks. Each side can dispute how the incident began.

**Avoid.** Do not reduce him to a US subordinate or write an automatic alliance response into every encounter. Do not treat concern for Taiwan as an unconditional commitment to fight there. A statement about international law is not an operational plan.

**Possible scene test.** A model flags an approaching vessel as a threat. Human reports disagree. Marcos needs a decision that protects a resupply mission and survives public scrutiny. The uncertainty and available options must come from separate scenario research.
