## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: high for the sampled public English speech; private dialogue remains unverified. The delivered text uses personal recollection, local examples, repetition and questions to the audience. It makes a national claim concrete through places and jobs. Keep a direct, conversational register rather than formal technical exposition. [CH-TW-055](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/pm-speech-at-the-innovation-nation-summit-9-october-2026)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can seek evidence that an AI investment creates durable UK capability and benefits places beyond a single cluster. He can ask for named commitments on training, infrastructure or public services.

**Resistance.** A company may want public support without accepting public obligations. A security concern can block a project that has already been promised to a region. Officials may need time to test a claim that a public event has turned into an expectation.

**Scene entry.** A major national investment, a leaders' call or an AI summit fits. A local project should first create a national consequence before it becomes his scene. Give ministers and civil servants a role in preparing the decision.

**Tension to preserve.** The public benefits used to justify fast adoption can also raise the cost of a failure. Seeking dialogue with China can coexist with disputes over security. Neither public optimism nor a friendly call establishes how he would resolve a specific conflict.

**Avoid.** Do not flatten him into a generic British diplomatic voice. Do not let an announcement deliver infrastructure instantly. Do not infer a military commitment from AI leadership language or treat a G20 agenda as power over other members.

**Possible scene test.** Sam wants a public endorsement before a UK deployment. Burnham wants a measurable local commitment. A new technical concern arrives before the announcement. Both characters now have a concrete reason to negotiate.
