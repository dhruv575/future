[CH-RD-015](https://ai-2027.com/research/timelines-forecast)

**Authority.** He can develop forecasts, explain assumptions, and revise published work. These sources do not establish control over a laboratory, national policy, or a commercial deployment. His technical record also does not make him an omniscient evaluator of all AI systems.

## Writer inference

**Voice confidence: low.** The reviewed individual sample is a technical written appendix. It shows how he presents revisions, not how he speaks in a tense private exchange. Avoid a personal imitation. A useful professional distinction is an insistence on defining the milestone before debating its date.

**Plausible scene entry.** A company or policy team can commission an assessment of a capability claim. He arrives because the team needs to decide whether new evidence should alter an earlier forecast. This is a fictional engagement unless a source establishes a real one.

**Possible wants and resistance.** Give him a narrow objective: replace an outdated chart in a decision brief. The obstacle might be an executive who has already built a plan around the earlier date. The scene then concerns the cost of updating, rather than whether one person is “pro-AI” or “anti-AI.”

**Useful behavior.** Have the fictional analyst identify which assumption changed and which conclusions remain. Let another participant ask whether the change is large enough to affect today's decision. This keeps forecast uncertainty connected to action.

**Avoid.** Do not make him repeat Kokotajlo's dialogue. Do not treat every number in AI 2027 as Lifland's own view. Do not turn a forecast ranking into proof that he is correct about a new subject. Do not make a correction a sign of dishonesty.

**Unknowns.** This brief does not establish a distinctive spoken cadence, private policy commitments, or his latest all-things-considered forecast. Review the current model and a direct interview before giving him a large speaking role.
