## Evidence

**Role at the source dates.** a16z identifies Schimpf as Anduril cofounder and CEO in interviews published in March 2024 and January 2025. This is distinct from Palmer Luckey's founder role. These dated sources do not, alone, verify every later change in corporate authority. [CH-RD-041](https://a16z.com/securing-americas-future-how-technology-companies-and-washington-are-building-a-safer-world/), [CH-RD-042](https://a16z.com/how-ai-is-changing-warfare-anduril-ceo-brian-schimpf/)

**Public position in 2024.** Schimpf emphasizes the importance of new suppliers being able to produce at meaningful scale. He discusses autonomous systems, changing operational demands, and US leadership. He also identifies limits on what he can disclose about a program. His ability to discuss a contract is not the same as authority to reveal all its details. [CH-RD-041](https://a16z.com/securing-americas-future-how-technology-companies-and-washington-are-building-a-safer-world/)

**Public position in 2025.** He separates how the government buys from what it should buy. He criticizes procurement incentives and long development cycles. At the same time, he says a company must understand its customer and succeed within the system it faces. He does not make complete institutional reform a prerequisite for business execution. [CH-RD-042](https://a16z.com/how-ai-is-changing-warfare-anduril-ceo-brian-schimpf/)

**Tension and limits.** The interviews join a claim about national need to a company's need to win orders. Both matter. Neither proves insincerity. These are investor-hosted discussions, so product claims and market judgments need separate verification before becoming facts about the game's world.

## Writer inference

**Public voice.** His answers are practical and explanatory. He breaks a broad problem into separate questions, uses commercial comparisons, and returns to adoption and scale. He can criticize a system while still discussing how to work through it. This differs from Luckey's more conspicuous public analogies. [CH-RD-042](https://a16z.com/how-ai-is-changing-warfare-anduril-ceo-brian-schimpf/)

**Plausible scene entry.** Use a negotiation about deployment, production, or an integration that has to work under real constraints. State why the CEO is needed rather than a product lead. A large commitment or unresolved contract term can justify his presence.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version could want the customer to choose a stable requirement and commit to a volume. The resistance could be uncertainty about the mission, a testing concern, or funding that arrives too late to support production.

**Useful conflict.** Let him ask which deliverable is actually being purchased. Another character may want an open-ended promise of future capability. That disagreement creates a concrete decision about schedule, scope, or accountability.

**Avoid.** Do not make him a duplicate of Luckey. Do not write him as having authority to approve the use of force. Do not assume that building an autonomous system means supporting every possible level of autonomy.

**Unknowns.** These sources do not reveal private relationships, internal disagreements among founders, or classified product behavior. Recheck his role for later game years. A current direct interview would improve confidence about how his public emphasis has changed since 2025.
