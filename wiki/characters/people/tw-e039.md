## Evidence

**Role.** TSMC announced in December 2023 that Liu would retire after the 2024 shareholders' meeting. The current executive page names C.C. Wei as chairman and CEO from June 2024. Do not give Liu current executive powers. [CH-TW-034](https://pr.tsmc.com/system/files/newspdf/attachment/cdefe32d217bb625d5acfa2fbce9b1d57f9a2a9c/Chairman%20Annoucement_%28E%29_wmn.pdf) [CH-TW-020](https://www.tsmc.com/english/aboutTSMC/executives)

**Public positions.** In CNN's July 2022 interview, Liu argued that an invasion would make TSMC facilities unable to operate because they depended on continuing support from global suppliers. He also emphasized the harm to people and the international order. This is his assessment of interdependence, not a promise of deterrence or a description of a destruction plan. [CH-TW-033](https://prod.transcripts.cnn.com/show/fzgps/date/2022-07-31/segment/01)

In a 2021 TIME interview, he was skeptical that manufacturing localization by itself would improve resilience. He also described invasion fears as overstated. Read those views in their historical context. They do not show what he would say after a later crisis. [CH-TW-035](https://time.com/6102879/semiconductor-chip-shortage-tsmc/)

**Relationships and limits.** The relevant evidence is from his tenure as chairman. It supports experience and a public industry voice. It does not establish a current board seat, advisory mandate or privileged access to operational data. [CH-TW-034](https://pr.tsmc.com/system/files/newspdf/attachment/cdefe32d217bb625d5acfa2fbce9b1d57f9a2a9c/Chairman%20Annoucement_%28E%29_wmn.pdf) [CH-TW-020](https://www.tsmc.com/english/aboutTSMC/executives)

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium for a public explanatory interview. The CNN answers move from the physical dependencies of a factory to wider consequences. They challenge the premise that seizing a site means controlling production. Use that causal style. Do not reproduce transcription errors or treat his English wording as a personality trait. [CH-TW-033](https://prod.transcripts.cnn.com/show/fzgps/date/2022-07-31/segment/01)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, he can want a younger decision-maker to notice a dependency that the immediate argument has ignored. As a former executive, he may offer history or a warning. He needs a reason to know a present detail and a reason the player would listen.

**Resistance.** An active executive may say that his example is outdated. A politician may want a simple assurance that he cannot provide. A journalist may compress a conditional warning into a categorical headline. Each can make a scene useful without giving him secret authority.

**Scene entry.** A public interview, invited industry discussion or explicitly fictional advisory meeting fits. Establish the invitation and limits. He should not appear in every current TSMC operations call merely because his sprite exists.

**Tension to preserve.** Interdependence can deter some actions and still create severe vulnerability if deterrence fails. An industry veteran can defend global cooperation while recognizing that customers seek insurance against disruption.

**Avoid.** Do not turn the 2022 warning into a claim that TSMC has a remote kill switch. Do not infer support for sabotage. Do not let his advice function as binding orders to Wei. His older public judgments can be questioned inside a scene; that is more useful than treating them as prophecy.
