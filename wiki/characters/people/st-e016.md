## Evidence

**Role and professional history.** RAND's live biography identifies Doll as a senior political scientist and professor of policy analysis. Her work includes military capability, strategy, logistics, and Indo-Pacific scenarios. It records past service as co-director of RAND's Center for Gaming and an analytic liaison to US Army Pacific. These are research and liaison roles, not a military command. [CH-RD-032](https://www.rand.org/about/people/d/doll_abby.html)

**Research relationship.** She coauthored the April 2026 Breakwater overview with Karl P. Mueller. The game explores how beliefs about AI development could affect incentives to take preventive action against a rival. The page describes initial observations and hypotheses. It does not provide a calibrated probability of war. Only its public summary was reviewed here. [CH-RD-034](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4703-1.html)

**Limits and tension.** A designed exercise can expose a problem worth studying without proving that a real leader would choose the same action. The sponsor's question, participant selection, information, and adjudication all matter. A writer should preserve the distinction between an exercise result and a prediction.

## Writer inference

**Voice confidence: low.** The biography and joint publication do not establish an individual conversational style. Use clear professional dialogue about scenario design and practical military constraints. Do not turn the paper's prose into a personal voice.

**Plausible scene entry.** She can appear as a game designer or invited analyst presenting what an exercise did and did not reveal. Establish the question the exercise was commissioned to explore.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version might need participants to specify what their proposed move is meant to achieve. An official could prefer a vague instruction that hides a difficult tradeoff. The scene can make that tradeoff visible.

**Useful conflict.** A participant claims that a quick technical action will produce a strategic outcome. The analyst asks what intervening events and decisions the plan depends on. This is professional role guidance, not a claim about Doll's private behavior.

**Avoid.** Do not make her personally endorse an action because a player selected it. Do not infer secret operational knowledge from a prior liaison role. Do not have a tabletop outcome settle a policy dispute without further argument.

**Unknowns.** An individual interview, lecture, or recorded briefing is needed for a larger speaking part. Private priorities, access to specific officials, and a future role in 2035 remain unknown.
