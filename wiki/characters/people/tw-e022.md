## Evidence

**Role.** AMD's current profile names Su chair and CEO. This brief uses that dated observation and does not assume she remains in the office in 2035. [CH-TW-030](https://www.amd.com/en/corporate/leadership/lisa-su.html)

**Public positions.** In prepared May 2025 Senate testimony, Su argued that US AI leadership required computing infrastructure, energy, faster innovation and open ecosystems. She described hardware, software and models from different suppliers as able to work together. The testimony links AMD's interests to US industrial and security aims. It is advocacy, not an independent evaluation of AMD's products. [CH-TW-031](https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2025/preserving-us-leadership-in-the-race-for-ai.html)

In an edited 2024 TIME interview, she described technology investment over long periods and rejected a short-term view of the AI opportunity. Her answer emphasized choosing a direction and building the necessary capabilities. This was a business judgment, not a forecast that every AI investment would succeed. [CH-TW-032](https://time.com/7026241/lisa-su-amd-ceo-interview/)

**Relationships and authority.** Her testimony identifies Microsoft and OpenAI among AMD's collaborators. Her role supports company strategy. A partnership announcement does not transfer another firm's board powers or guarantee that all products are interchangeable. [CH-TW-030](https://www.amd.com/en/corporate/leadership/lisa-su.html) [CH-TW-031](https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2025/preserving-us-leadership-in-the-race-for-ai.html)

## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium. The edited interview gives direct answers that connect long-term choices to specific work. Use this sequence, with few slogans. [CH-TW-032](https://time.com/7026241/lisa-su-amd-ceo-interview/)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, she can seek a real design win: engineering access, software effort, a volume commitment and a route from trial to production. She can offer an alternative to an incumbent supplier while asking the buyer to do the work that makes the alternative viable.

**Resistance.** Sam may want a second supplier but refuse to divert engineers to qualify it. A customer may compare headline chip performance while ignoring software changes or rack integration. A public official may demand rapid domestic capacity on a schedule that does not match manufacturing.

**Scene entry.** Use a qualification review, a supply negotiation or an industrial-policy hearing. Her presence should change a commitment or resolve a major obstacle. Routine troubleshooting can stay with engineering teams.

**Tension to preserve.** Open interfaces can help win adoption and still require substantial vendor-specific work. A long investment horizon can justify patience but cannot answer every delivery failure. Give her a milestone the player can verify later.

**Avoid.** Do not use a family relationship to Huang as a substitute for corporate interests. Do not present AMD hardware as an instant, cost-free swap. Do not equate a public growth thesis with indifference to safety or security. Neither a private geopolitical loyalty nor an exact view of a hypothetical rogue model follows from these sources.

**Possible scene test.** The player can secure future supply only by moving engineers off a launch. Su needs that commitment before she can give a credible schedule. Both accepting and refusing have understandable costs.
