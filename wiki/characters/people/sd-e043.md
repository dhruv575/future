## Evidence

**Role.** President of the European Commission on its live profile. The page describes a second term through 2029. It says she sets the Commission agenda, allocates portfolios and represents the Commission internationally. It does not give her command of member states' armed forces. [CH-TW-057](https://commission.europa.eu/about/organisation/president/about-president_en)

**Public positions.** At the February 2025 AI Action Summit, she promoted large-scale investment, European technical capacity and access for firms beyond the largest companies. She framed the effort around cooperation and talent. The announced capital target is not evidence that all funds were raised or spent. [CH-TW-058](https://luxembourg.representation.ec.europa.eu/actualites-et-evenements/actualites/eu-launches-investai-initiative-mobilise-eur200-billion-investment-artificial-intelligence-2025-02-11_en)

At the July EU-China summit, she and António Costa represented the EU, with Kaja Kallas participating. The EU account combines engagement with demands on trade, data flows and security. It also opposes force or coercion changing the Taiwan Strait status quo. These are institutional positions recorded by the EU. [CH-TW-059](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/meetings/international-summit/2025/07/24/)

**Authority and relationships.** Her office can set a Commission program and coordinate commissioners. National governments remain separate actors. Costa represents a different institution. Their presence in the same meeting does not merge their powers. ## Writer inference

**Voice.** Confidence: medium for formal announcements, low for private conversation. The attributed AI remarks move from a public benefit to a specifically European method and then a funded initiative. Use a concrete proposal and a political reason for it. Do not make every line a regulatory warning. [CH-TW-058](https://luxembourg.representation.ec.europa.eu/actualites-et-evenements/actualites/eu-launches-investai-initiative-mobilise-eur200-billion-investment-artificial-intelligence-2025-02-11_en)

**Plausible wants.** In fiction, she can seek a commitment that enlarges European capacity and reduces a vulnerable dependency. An AI company can be a partner and still face conditions. Give the negotiation an object: access to compute, a research program, a transparency obligation or a supply agreement.

**Resistance.** Member governments may differ about cost and location. A firm may seek flexible access while officials seek predictable service and enforceable terms. Another major power may frame the European initiative as choosing sides.

**Scene entry.** Use a large investment announcement, a Commission policy decision or a formal international summit. For a narrow compliance question, staff and the relevant commissioner should normally appear first.

**Tension to preserve.** Building capacity can require foreign suppliers. An open innovation goal can conflict with scarce resources or security conditions. A scene should identify which promise is being negotiated rather than presenting Europe as a single moral position.

**Avoid.** Do not portray her as president of a unitary European state. Do not let her declare a military response on behalf of all members. Do not equate regulation with opposition to AI investment. Do not treat a public funding target as immediately available cash.

**Possible scene test.** The Commission can support an emergency compute project, but member governments want access and local benefits. Sam wants a single customer. She needs a proposal that can survive the institutions behind her.
