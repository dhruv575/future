## Evidence

**Role and authority.** CSET's live biography identifies Bresnick as a research fellow and Andrew W. Marshall Fellow. It describes work on military AI, US–China technology competition, and Chinese technology policy. It records earlier research at Carnegie China and public-policy briefings. This supports an analyst role, not a government spokesperson or intelligence-command role. [CH-RD-008](https://cset.georgetown.edu/staff/sam-bresnick/)

**Research finding.** His June 2024 report examines 59 Chinese-language articles published from 2020 through 2022. The public summary identifies concerns among Chinese defense writers about data, testing, communications, cybersecurity, and reliable AI. It challenges the idea that Chinese military researchers are uniformly unconcerned about AI failures. [CH-RD-009](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/chinas-military-ai-roadblocks/)

**Limits.** The reviewed page is the executive summary. It is not an inventory of all Chinese deployments or classified programs. A literature sample can show what selected authors discuss. It cannot establish what Xi Jinping privately believes or what a military unit can do today. [CH-RD-009](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/chinas-military-ai-roadblocks/)

**Relationships and tension.** The institutional record places him in policy research and public briefings. It does not establish a personal link to the game's executives. The substantive tension is that China can have strong ambitions and serious implementation barriers at the same time. Neither point cancels the other.

## Writer inference

**Voice confidence: low.** No extended conversational sample was reviewed. Use professional, source-conscious dialogue rather than an invented personal manner. His distinctive contribution should be the provenance of an assertion: which Chinese publication says it, when, and what that publication can support.

**Plausible scene entry.** A policy aide can ask him to assess a document being used to justify a new restriction. His access should come from an invited briefing, not unexplained participation in an internal company meeting.

**Possible wants.** Give him a precise task: correct a briefing that merges ambition, procurement, and demonstrated capability into one claim. He may need a sentence changed before an official relies on it.

**Possible resistance.** The recipient needs a decision today. A broad warning is easier to communicate than a qualified finding. That conflict can create useful dialogue without making the analyst indecisive.

**Avoid.** Do not make him the voice of “what China thinks.” Do not have him read a classified Chinese plan from open literature. Do not treat a source's concern as proof that the corresponding failure is already occurring.

**Unknowns.** This brief does not establish private political preferences, current access to policymakers, a distinctive spoken voice, or personal relations with Chinese officials. A substantial speaking role needs an additional first-person interview or hearing transcript.
