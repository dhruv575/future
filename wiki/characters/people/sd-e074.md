It is too thin to reproduce his spontaneous speech or emotional response under pressure. **Plausible scene entry.** A delivery crisis, capacity review, or cross-business resource decision can justify involving him. A generic debate about AI's future may be better handled by a technical or policy specialist unless the story establishes why he has a direct stake.

**Possible wants and resistance.** A fictional version might seek a realistic schedule that protects several obligations at once. The obstacle could be a customer demanding priority, a component shortage, or a proposed change that requires further qualification. Establish those conditions in the fiction rather than implying they are current RTX problems.

**Useful conflict.** Make the player confront two legitimate commitments. A change that helps one customer can delay another. The character's concern can be execution and credibility without assuming that profit is his only motive.

**Avoid.** Do not give him a detailed AI-governance doctrine that these sources do not contain. Do not treat a company result as a description of his personality. Do not make him directly command a weapons system in service.

**Unknowns.** A longer individual interview is required before a large speaking role. Private preferences, crisis conduct, relationships with other leaders, and a precise future AI responsibility remain unverified. The failed earnings-call PDF was not used for voice claims.
